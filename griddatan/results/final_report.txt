================= FINAL VERDICT =================
On this project's verified, parity-anchored corpus (dataset_size=3000), griddatan
('nearest') loses to the Gaussian Process surrogate decisively and consistently across every
axis tested:
  - NMSE: griddatan performs WORSE than predicting the mean (NMSE>1); the GP's worst of 307
    configs still beats griddatan by 5.9x; its best beats it by 249x.
  - Gamma recovery: griddatan is off by a mean of 1.53 decades (~34x); the GP recovers gamma1
    to a mean error of 0.29 decades (~2.0x) on the identical full 1000-row holdout.
  - Joint recovery: griddatan's 2-D loss surface collapses to a flat plateau, with 99.7% of
    rows recovering the same fixed point regardless of the true data -- not a usable estimate
    at all. No comparable GP number was computed (see Part 3's caveat).
  - Root cause, independently confirmed (Milestone 9): griddatan's raw, UNSTANDARDIZED feature
    distance is dominated by delta/epsilon (scale ~10-100) over gamma1/lambda1 (scale ~1e-5 to
    1e-1) by multiple orders of magnitude -- not a tuning problem, a structural one, inherent
    to how griddatan (as actually built by this project's own interpolant.m starting point)
    computes nearest-neighbor distance.

This is the honest answer to the original ask: a gridded interpolant was infeasible (no full
rectangular grid in the data), griddatan was the next viable option, and griddatan -- evaluated
rigorously, under adversarial review at every step -- does not work either, for a specific,
confirmed, reportable reason.
